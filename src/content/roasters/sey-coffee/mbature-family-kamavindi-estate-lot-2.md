## processing

Hand-picked at peak ripeness, hand-sorted, and floated to remove defects.
De-pulped on the day of harvest, dry-fermented for 18–20 hours in darkness,
washed and turned repeatedly in fresh water, then dry-fermented for an
additional 24 hours. Washed again, floated through channels to sort by density,
and dried on raised beds under shade nets to 16% moisture before finishing on
upper beds at ~10.5%.

## farm

Kamavindi was founded in 1958 by the late John Njiru Mbature during British
colonial rule. It began as a 20-hectare (~50-acre) farm with just 100 coffee
trees, the legal limit for native Africans at the time. When restrictions were
lifted in 1961, John planted an additional 500 trees. Kamavindi grew to 10,000
trees: 7,000 SL28 and 3,000 Ruiru 11.

It is now managed by Peter Mbature, his mother Hellen Weveti, and his sister
Gladwell Wanjira. The farm is transitioning fully to SL28 and establishing a
training center for farmers and coffee buyers on processing and farm
management.

## about SL*

SEY released this selection last year as SL34. Recent genetic testing on this
farm and others has challenged assumptions about which varieties are actually
cultivated in Kenya; SEY now believes this lot comprises SL34 and SL9, and calls
it an ongoing research project.
